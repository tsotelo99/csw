      <h2 id="crossings-intro-title">Indigenous histories of movement and connection</h2>
    </div>
    <p>We live in a world of seemingly unprecedented motion. As climate change, political struggles, and ethnic cleansing propel humans and their other-than-human kin across borders in new ways, we find ourselves stymied by a failure of historical imagination. Historians joined others for generations in seeing the past as prologue to a state- and settler-centered modernity, a world increasingly (some might say inevitably) fixed in place by the enclosure, erasure, and transformation of Indigenous and nonstate spaces. This way of framing past, present, and future has created blind spots, especially now that our assumptions of a clearly demarcated, state-centered future are being tested.</p>

      <p class="about-eyebrow">A deeper archive</p>
      <h2 id="crossings-archive-title">How can Indigenous history speak to a changing world?</h2>
      <p>How might Indigenous history speak to our current age of global change? How might we recover a larger global “archive” tied to a deeper, Native-centered past of human and non-human mobility and border crossings? For centuries, peoples, plants, animals, germs, ideas, and objects have moved across land and sea. These deeper histories offer stories that defy nation-state borders and binary framings of human identity, and reveal relations of connection and exchange that have been long obscured by nation-state-centered histories.</p>
      <p>What can these histories, particularly those grounded in an Indigenous long durée, tell us about exchanges, relationships, and innovations across global borderlands and geographies? And how can Indigenous-centered patterns of migration, sanctuary, and colonization across the globe speak to—and help us reframe—our historical approach to homelands, borderlands, crossings, and identities in ways that will matter to future generations?</p>
    </div>
  </section>

      <p class="about-eyebrow">Workshops</p>
      <h2 id="crossings-workshops-title">Conversations across places</h2>
      <p>Anchored to a deeper planetary archive linked to Native nations and Indigenous communities, this work connects global Indigenous studies with Native-centered scholarship on place, mobility, climate change and justice, human and other-than-human kinship, migration and sanctuary, borderlands and border crossings, Native transnational relationships and social movements, and Indigenous politics, identities, diplomacy, and cross-community relations in historical contexts of global change.</p>
      <p>The workshop will result in an edited volume of essays. The original call for papers requested abstracts of around 350 words and a two-page CV or resume by March 1, 2025; accepted papers of 7,000–10,000 words were due August 15, 2025, for circulation ahead of the symposium. Travel and accommodation support was to be made available. For more information, contact <a href="mailto:cntrsw@unm.edu">cntrsw@unm.edu</a>.</p>
